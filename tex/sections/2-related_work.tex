\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

\subsection{Stereophotoclinometry methods}
\label{subsec:spc-methods}

SPC combines stereo geometry with photoclinometric constraints to reconstruct surface topography and relative albedo from optical images. \citet{gaskell2008} established its application to small-body characterization and navigation using local terrain patches, or maplets, and global shape products. Stereo observations constrain the three-dimensional positions of surface landmarks, while brightness variations across images acquired under different viewing and illumination conditions constrain local slopes and albedo. The estimated slopes are integrated into elevations using geometric and topographic constraints, including stereo-derived heights, limb observations, and overlapping terrain maps \citep{gaskell2023}. These complementary constraints connect local surface detail to the larger-scale shape of the body.

Operational SPC reconstruction proceeds from a coarse initial shape toward progressively finer terrain products. The initial model supports image registration and maplet construction; overlapping maplets subsequently provide regional digital terrain models (DTMs) and a global surface representation \citep{barnouin2020,palmer2022}. The mathematical formulation of \citet{gaskell2023} separates the estimation into interacting iterative solutions for maplet topography, landmark positions, and spacecraft position and pointing. As higher-resolution images become available, finer maplets are initialized from the existing terrain and incorporated into subsequent updates \citep{palmer2022}. Reconstruction quality therefore depends on both the observations supplied and their registration and use throughout the refinement process.

Recent work extends the geometric and photometric estimation underlying this process. \citet{driver2025} introduced Photoclinometry-from-Motion (PhoMo), which incorporates photoclinometric constraints into a keypoint-based structure-from-motion framework. Its factor graph jointly estimates camera poses, landmark positions, Sun-relative directions, surface normals, and albedos, reducing reliance on manual maplet alignment. AstroSplat incorporates planetary reflectance models into 2D Gaussian splatting, explicitly relating image intensity to illumination, surface orientation, and albedo \citep{nolan2026astrosplat}. These developments address reconstruction from supplied imagery and provide complementary advances to the problem of deciding which observations to acquire.

\subsection{Image effectiveness and reconstruction accuracy}
\label{subsec:spc-image-effectiveness-accuracy}

The usefulness of an SPC image depends on the constraints it adds to the available image set. Practical observation recommendations distinguish images that emphasize topography from those that help separate albedo variations from shading \citep{palmer2022}. \citet{palmer2024geometry} tested this complementarity through controlled reconstructions of synthetic terrain. In their local-terrain experiments, four topographic images bracketing the target from north, south, east, and west, together with one albedo image acquired near local noon, produced high-quality DTMs. Their image-replication experiment also found little improvement from increasing the number of inputs without introducing new observing geometries. These results support the selection of complementary images, although the five-image configuration depends on the tested terrain, geometry, resolution, and processing conditions and does not prescribe an image budget for global mapping.

Evidence from actual asteroid imagery further links image selection to reconstruction outcomes. \citet{zhang2025imagepairs} used KD-tree searches to determine overlapping image pairs in unordered Bennu, Vesta, and Ryugu archives and support selection under SPC observing constraints. In a local Vesta experiment, 70 candidate images were reduced to 22 overlapping images and then to five images with complementary viewing and illumination geometries. The resulting SPC terrain model had a grid spacing of 100 m and a reported elevation root-mean-square error of 61.16 m against a reference digital elevation model (DEM). This provides a reconstructed product with quantified geometric differences, although the reference DEM has its own uncertainty. Because selection takes place within an existing archive, any reduction in processing inputs occurs after the acquisition and downlink costs have already been incurred.

Assessing image effectiveness through the reconstructed model requires distinguishing internal consistency from geometric accuracy. \citet{weirich2022} reconstructed a synthetic asteroid from simulated images and compared internal SPC quality indicators with three-dimensional errors relative to known terrain. For flight data, \citet{alasad2021} validated successive Bennu models using stereo residuals, height dispersion among overlapping maplets, image comparisons, and independent laser-altimeter measurements. These assessments provide complementary evidence: stereo residuals measure agreement with image observations, while maplet dispersion measures consistency between overlapping local surfaces. Neither alone establishes agreement with the actual surface, since overlapping maplets can share systematic errors that remain undetected by their mutual agreement.

Controlled sensitivity studies clarify further limits of these diagnostics. \citet{palmer2024accuracy} evaluated terrain differences and image correlation under varied input and processing conditions, identifying errors in fine-scale relief and difficulties near steep slopes. Image correlation is useful when reference terrain is unavailable, but an incorrect photometric model can introduce errors in the vertical scale of local relief that escape both internal consistency checks and image correlation. Similarly, the rendering performance and agreement with reference reconstructions reported for PhoMo and AstroSplat provide evidence about different aspects of model quality \citep{driver2025,nolan2026astrosplat}. AstroSplat's geometric evaluation uses PhoMo outputs as a reference, so it does not independently establish absolute surface accuracy. Model resolution, photometric agreement, and geometric accuracy must therefore be evaluated separately, with known terrain or independent measurements providing external validation where available.

The distinction between image quantity and reconstruction benefit also matters for mission resources. Approximately 600 AMICA science images were analyzed during Hayabusa's characterization of Itokawa \citep{gaskell2008}, while Rosetta's SPC SHAP5 model of comet 67P incorporated more than 5500 OSIRIS narrow-angle camera images \citep{preusker2017}. Bennu models v10, v20, and v42 used 2351, 5114, and 15,325 images, respectively \citep{palmer2022}; these are image counts associated with successive products and should not be summed. At a smaller mission scale, the prospective Milani NavCam budget allocates approximately 200 MB to compressed asteroid imagery, corresponding to about 540 images for navigation and science \citep{ferrari2026navcam}. These examples illustrate the range of imaging requirements and the resource constraints facing observation selection.

Large SPC imaging campaigns involve extensive repeated coverage, as illustrated by Bennu's Approach mosaics and seven-pass Baseball Diamond campaign \citep{palmer2022}. Although part of this repetition provides necessary operational margin, repeatedly imaging already constrained terrain under similar viewing and illumination conditions can accumulate substantial redundancy while yielding little additional reconstruction benefit \citep{palmer2024geometry}. At the scale of thousands of images, such acquisitions consume observing time, storage, downlink capacity, and processing effort that could instead support unresolved regions. Selecting a compact image subset after downlink cannot recover these acquisition and transmission costs \citep{zhang2025imagepairs}. Observation planning is therefore essential to reduce redundancy before images are acquired. It should use the current reconstruction and regional quality assessment to prioritize complementary observations, avoid repeated acquisitions with negligible accuracy gains, and stop further imaging for the reconstruction objective once the required model quality is achieved.

\subsection{Observation planning for small bodies}
\label{subsec:spc-observation-planning}

Small-body observation planning must translate reconstruction requirements into decisions about spacecraft motion, camera pointing, and image acquisition under visibility, illumination, and operational constraints. \citet{pesce2018mapping} formulated autonomous navigation and mapping as a partially observable decision problem and selected observation orbits using a reward based on surface-facet imaging geometry and observation history. \citet{chan2019mapping} extended this direction through deep reinforcement learning, coordinating maneuvering, imaging, and downlink decisions under memory and operational constraints. These frameworks explicitly account for the evolving map or observation history. Their numerical mapping objectives, however, use accumulated geometric observation scores rather than regional terrain errors measured after successive SPC reconstructions.

Focusing on acquisition timing, \citet{piccinin2022} learned whether to capture an image along prescribed spacecraft motion and pointing. The policy uses observing geometry, previous observations, mapping statistics, and remaining storage, and was evaluated on Eros, Itokawa, Bennu, and comet 67P. In one 67P simulation, a learned policy acquired 285 images compared with 500 for uniform scheduling, a 43\% reduction, while improving the geometric mapping index. This demonstrates the potential for selective imaging under an observation budget. The index combines angular suitability and image-count information, however, so the reported gain does not directly measure the surface accuracy obtained by applying SPC to the selected images.

\citet{wang2025} addressed the joint selection of observing positions and imaging actions from a low-resolution shape model. Their method combines a genetic algorithm with local search to balance geometrically effective coverage against the numbers of observing positions and imaging actions, accounting for illumination, visibility, and repeated imaging requirements. Monte Carlo evaluation under position and pointing perturbations improves the robustness of the selected plan. This provides a direct precedent for satisfying SPC-inspired observing constraints with fewer positions and images. Its reported coverage and geometry statistics assess the suitability of the planned inputs, while the regional accuracy of a newly reconstructed SPC surface is not used to select the next observation round.

Related next-best-view research provides tools for adapting observations to an evolving reconstruction. PC-NBV predicts candidate-view coverage gains from partial point clouds \citep{zeng2020}, SCONE estimates coverage gains through occupancy and visibility prediction \citep{guedon2022}, and GenNBV learns observation policies across reconstruction environments \citep{chen2024}. Beyond coverage, \citet{frahm2025vinnbv} introduced VIN-NBV to predict relative reconstruction improvement from candidate views, using training targets obtained by measuring reductions in Chamfer distance after incorporating additional observations. Quality-driven sequential view selection therefore already has a close precedent. Its RGB-D reconstruction formulation, however, does not model the coupled illumination, albedo, and terrain estimation required by SPC.

Our method integrates SPC reconstruction and regional accuracy assessment into a deep reinforcement learning planner, linking observation selection to the quality of the resulting surface model. Extending small-body planners evaluated primarily through observation geometry and coverage \citep{piccinin2022,wang2025}, the policy uses the current surface and regional diagnostics to identify unresolved terrain, then assesses the benefit of acquired images through quality changes after the next SPC update. This feedback provides a basis for prioritizing complementary observations and reducing redundant acquisition before imaging and downlink resources are consumed, consistent with the image-complementarity findings of \citet{palmer2024geometry}. Building on reconstruction-driven view selection \citep{frahm2025vinnbv}, our formulation evaluates observation benefits through SPC reconstruction, accounting for the coupled effects of illumination, albedo, and terrain. Following established SPC validation principles \citep{alasad2021,weirich2022}, final surface models are evaluated against reference terrain in simulation alongside image count. The method thus connects adaptive observation planning, regional model improvement, and validated geometric accuracy, enabling observation savings to be assessed at comparable reconstruction quality.
