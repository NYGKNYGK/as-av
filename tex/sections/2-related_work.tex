\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

SPC reconstructs surface topography and relative albedo by combining stereo geometry with image shading. \citet{gaskell2008} demonstrated its use for small-body characterization and navigation, representing local terrain with maplets and combining these patches into global shape models. \citet{gaskell2023} formalized the coupled estimation of maplet topography, landmark positions, and spacecraft position and pointing, using stereo, limb, and overlapping-maplet constraints to anchor elevations recovered from photometric slopes. \citet{barnouin2020} and \citet{palmer2022} described mission workflows that register images to an initial shape, iteratively refine local terrain, and introduce finer maplets as higher-resolution imagery becomes available. More recently, \citet{driver2025} introduced Photoclinometry-from-Motion (PhoMo), incorporating photoclinometric constraints into a keypoint-based structure-from-motion framework. Its factor graph jointly estimates camera poses, landmark positions, Sun-relative directions, surface normals, and albedos, reducing reliance on manual maplet alignment.

Studies of SPC image effectiveness examine how viewing geometry, illumination, and input errors affect the accuracy of reconstructed terrain. \citet{alasad2021} validated successive Bennu shape models using stereo residuals, height dispersion among overlapping maplets, comparisons between observed and rendered images, and independent laser-altimeter measurements. \citet{weirich2022} reconstructed a synthetic asteroid from simulated images and compared internal SPC quality indicators with three-dimensional errors relative to the known surface, providing a controlled assessment of what those indicators reveal about geometric accuracy. \citet{palmer2024accuracy} varied input images and processing conditions in synthetic-terrain experiments, evaluating terrain differences and image correlation to identify errors in fine-scale relief and near steep slopes. They also showed that an incorrect photometric model can distort the vertical scale of local relief without being exposed by internal consistency or image-correlation checks. Focusing on observation geometry, \citet{palmer2024geometry} tested combinations of viewing directions, illumination conditions, and image counts. Four complementary topographic images bracketing the target from north, south, east, and west, together with one albedo image near local noon, produced high-quality digital terrain models in their experiments. Replicating images without adding new observing geometries yielded little improvement, demonstrating the importance of complementary constraints within an image set; the tested five-image configuration applies to local terrain under the evaluated conditions.

Observation planning for SPC translates reconstruction requirements into choices of observing orbits, camera pointing, and image acquisition. \citet{pesce2018mapping} formulated autonomous small-body navigation and mapping as a partially observable decision problem and selected sequences of observing orbits through a reduced space of orbital macro-actions. Their reward accumulates surface-facet scores derived from emission and incidence angles, solar and spacecraft azimuths, and observation history, favoring coverage with geometries suited to SPC. \citet{wang2025} used a low-resolution shape model to formulate joint selection of observing positions and imaging actions under illumination, visibility, and repeated-observation constraints. They combined a genetic algorithm with local search to reduce the numbers of positions and images while maintaining geometrically effective coverage, and incorporated Monte Carlo sampling of position and pointing perturbations to improve plan robustness. Complementing acquisition planning, \citet{zhang2025imagepairs} used KD-tree searches to identify overlapping images in unordered asteroid archives and select inputs satisfying SPC observing constraints. Their local Vesta example reduced 70 candidates to 22 overlapping images and then to five images with complementary geometries, followed by SPC reconstruction and comparison with a reference terrain model. This selection operates after acquisition, whereas the orbit and imaging planners optimize geometric observation criteria without using regional errors from successive SPC reconstructions to choose subsequent observations.

Reinforcement learning has been applied to observation planning in small-body mapping, active three-dimensional reconstruction, and Earth observation. \citet{chan2019mapping} combined deep reinforcement learning with navigation and mapping models to coordinate spacecraft maneuvers, imaging, and downlink under onboard memory and operational constraints. \citet{piccinin2022} compared Neural Fitted Q and Deep Q Network policies for deciding whether to acquire an image along prescribed spacecraft motion and pointing. Their policies use observing geometry, previous observations, mapping statistics, and remaining storage, and were evaluated on Eros, Itokawa, Bennu, and comet 67P. In one 67P simulation, the learned policy acquired 285 images instead of 500 under uniform scheduling while improving the geometric mapping index. For active reconstruction, \citet{chen2024} proposed GenNBV, which learns a next-best-view policy over continuous camera positions and orientations using proximal policy optimization (PPO). The policy combines geometric occupancy, image features, and pose history, and rewards increases in surface coverage to support generalization to unseen objects and scenes. For agile Earth-observation satellites, \citet{chun2023scheduling} represented observation tasks and time-dependent attitude-transition constraints as a graph and trained a graph-attention decision network with PPO to construct profitable task sequences with short computation times. These applications motivate learned sequential observation policies, while our focus is to connect observation decisions to regional quality changes measured after SPC updates and evaluate image savings against final geometric accuracy.
